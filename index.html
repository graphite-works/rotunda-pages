Rotunda Privacy Policy

Last updated: September 22, 2026

Status: PM draft, not yet legally reviewed by counsel. This content is drafted for Rotunda's in-app privacy policy screen and this external, publicly reachable copy. Please do not treat any statement below as legal advice.
In short: Rotunda has no user accounts, no ads, and no analytics or tracking of any kind. There is no Rotunda-operated server. Everything the app shows you comes directly from official U.S. government sources over a secure connection, and everything the app remembers about you — your saved bills and votes — stays on your device. The one thing that ever leaves your device is the text you type into bill search, which goes straight to the government's own search service, not to us, because we have no "us" to send it to.

1. Who this policy covers

This policy covers the Rotunda mobile app (iOS and Android). Rotunda is a general-audience civic reference tool for looking up official U.S. Congressional records — recorded votes, bill text, and bill search — published by congress.gov, the U.S. Senate, and GovInfo (the Government Publishing Office's document repository). Rotunda does not operate any backend server of its own. There is nothing to log in to, nothing to sync, and no Rotunda-side database that stores information about you.

2. What Rotunda does not collect

  • No user accounts or login. There is nothing to register for and nothing tied to your identity anywhere in the app.
  • No advertising. No ad network, no ad identifiers, no ad SDK.
  • No analytics or tracking SDK of any kind. There is no analytics library, attribution SDK, or third-party tracker anywhere in the app.
  • No sale or sharing of personal information. Rotunda has never sold, rented, or shared personal information with any third party, and has no mechanism to do so.

3. What is stored on your device, and how to clear it

Rotunda stores a small amount of data locally on your device, using an on-device key-value store. None of it is transmitted anywhere, and none of it is visible to Rotunda's developers, because there is no server for it to be sent to.

What's stored on-device:

  • Saved / followed bills and votes — the bills and votes you've bookmarked, so Rotunda can show your Saved list and check those items for status changes.
  • A one-time notification-permission flag — a single true/false marker recording whether Rotunda has already shown you its in-app explanation of why it wants notification permission, so you're only asked once.
  • A local cache of recently-viewed search and vote results — kept so the app can show you the same screen instantly on a re-visit and can still show your last-known results if a government source is temporarily unreachable. Cached entries expire automatically after 24 hours.

How to clear it: you can remove an individual bill or vote from your Saved list at any time from within the app. To clear everything Rotunda has stored, uninstall the app — on-device storage is deleted along with the app, and reinstalling starts with nothing saved.

4. Bill search: the one thing sent off your device

When you search for a bill by keyword, Rotunda sends your search text directly to GovInfo's own public search API, over HTTPS, so it can look up matching bills. That request goes straight from your device to GovInfo's servers — it does not pass through, or get logged by, any Rotunda-operated server, because none exists. GovInfo's handling of that request is governed by GovInfo's own privacy policy, linked in Section 6 below.

Rotunda does not store your search history on your device beyond the short-lived result cache described in Section 3, and does not attach any identifier to a search that would let it, or GovInfo, tie searches together across sessions.

5. Local notifications

If you choose to enable notifications, Rotunda can notify you when a bill or vote you're following changes status. This is handled entirely on-device:

  • A background check runs periodically (roughly every 6 hours) to see whether anything you follow has changed, using the same government sources described in Section 6.
  • If something changed, Rotunda schedules a notification using your device's own local notification system.

This never involves a push notification service, a push token, or any server round-trip — it is a purely local, on-device schedule. Rotunda never sends your notification preferences, followed items, or device identifiers to anyone. You can decline the notification permission prompt, or turn notifications off at any time from your device's system Settings, and the app continues to work normally.

6. Government data sources Rotunda reads from

Every vote, bill, and document Rotunda shows you is read live, over HTTPS, directly from the U.S. government's own published sources. Rotunda does not operate a mirror, cache server, or intermediary for this data:

  • congress.gov — House recorded votes and bill metadata.
  • The U.S. Senate's own published records (senate.gov) — Senate recorded votes.
  • GovInfo, the Government Publishing Office's official document repository — bill text and bill search.

These requests use a Rotunda-provisioned API key that identifies the app to these services for rate-limiting purposes; it does not identify you personally, and it is not tied to your device or your activity in any way Rotunda controls.

Because these are independent, government-operated services, each has its own privacy practices, not Rotunda's:

  • congress.gov / Library of Congress: congress.gov/privacy-policy
  • U.S. Senate: senate.gov/legal/privacy_policy.htm
  • GovInfo / U.S. Government Publishing Office: govinfo.gov/privacy-policy

We encourage you to review those policies if you have questions about how those government services themselves handle requests.

7. Crash reporting (currently inactive)

Rotunda includes a crash-reporting library so that, once configured, crashes and errors can be diagnosed and fixed. As of the date of this policy, crash reporting is not active: no crash-reporting project has been configured, so the library initializes in a no-op mode and sends nothing, to anyone, today.

If crash reporting is activated in a future version of the app, what it would send is limited to technical diagnostic information used to fix bugs: error messages, stack traces, and basic device/OS information (device model, OS version, app version). It would not include anything you type into search, the contents of your Saved list, or any other information Rotunda does not otherwise collect. This policy will be updated, and the "last updated" date above revised, if and when that changes.

8. No sale or sharing of information

Rotunda has never sold, rented, traded, or shared personal information with any third party for advertising, marketing, or any other purpose, and has no technical mechanism to do so — there is no Rotunda-operated server where such information could even be collected in the first place. The only outbound data flow is the search request described in Section 4, sent directly to GovInfo as part of using the search feature itself, and the government API calls described in Section 6.

9. Children's privacy

Rotunda is a general-audience civic reference app. It is not directed at children, has no account system, no age gate, and no feature that asks for or knowingly collects personal information from anyone, including children under 13. Rotunda does not have actual knowledge that any of its users are children under 13. Because Rotunda collects no personal information from any user and has no server-side records of any kind, the Children's Online Privacy Protection Act (COPPA, 16 CFR Part 312) does not require a COPPA-specific notice for an app in this position. If you believe a child has provided personal information through this app, please contact us using the information in Section 11 and we will look into it — though as described throughout this policy, there is no mechanism by which the app itself would have collected or retained such information.

10. Your state privacy rights

A number of U.S. states (including California, under the CCPA/CPRA, and roughly 19 other states as of 2026) give residents rights such as the right to know what personal information is collected about them and the right to request deletion. Nearly all of these laws apply only to businesses that meet a revenue, user-count, or data-sale threshold. Rotunda has no revenue, sells no data, and keeps no server-side records about any user at all, so it does not meet any state's applicability threshold today.

That said, we want to be straightforward with you: because Rotunda stores nothing about you on any server, there is nothing for us to "know" or "delete" on your behalf beyond what already lives entirely on your own device (Section 3), which you can already view and clear yourself at any time, in the app or by uninstalling it. If you'd still like to reach out with a privacy question or request, see Section 11 — we'll honor a reasonable request voluntarily, even though no state law currently compels us to.

11. Contact

For questions about this policy or Rotunda's privacy practices, please reach out through the app's official support page: Support. A dedicated support/contact address has not been finalized yet. This section will be updated with the finalized contact channel before this policy is submitted to any app store, and the "last updated" date will be revised accordingly.

12. Security

All communication between Rotunda and any external source — the government APIs in Section 6 — happens exclusively over HTTPS (encrypted in transit). Rotunda has no server-side infrastructure of its own, so there is no Rotunda-operated database, backend, or account system to secure or to be breached. Data you store on your device (Section 3) is protected by your device's own operating-system-level security (e.g., device passcode/biometric lock and OS-level app sandboxing); Rotunda relies on those platform protections rather than adding its own encryption layer on top.

13. Changes to this policy

If Rotunda's data practices change — for example, if crash reporting (Section 7) is activated, or a new feature changes what is stored or where it's sent — this policy will be updated and the "last updated" date at the top will be revised to reflect that. Since Rotunda has no account system, we cannot notify you directly; we encourage you to check back periodically, and any in-app privacy policy screen will always reflect the current version of this document.
