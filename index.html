Rotunda Privacy Policy

This page is generated from the same source as the in-app Privacy Policy screen, so the two always agree.

In short

Rotunda keeps your activity to yourself. There are no user accounts, no ads, and no analytics or tracking of any kind. Everything the app shows you comes directly from official U.S. government sources over a secure connection, and everything the app remembers about you, your saved bills and votes, stays on your device under your control. The one thing that ever leaves your device is the text you type into bill search, and that goes straight to the government's own search service as part of looking up your results, not to Rotunda.

The rest of this document explains that in full, source by source.

1. Who this policy covers

This policy covers the Rotunda mobile app (Expo / React Native, iOS and Android). Rotunda is a general-audience civic reference tool for looking up official U.S. Congressional records: recorded votes, bill text, and bill search, published by congress.gov, the U.S. Senate, and GovInfo (the Government Publishing Office's document repository). Rotunda gives you direct access to those records without an account, a login, or a sync step: what you see and save is yours, kept locally, on your device.

2. What Rotunda does not collect

Rotunda has:

  • No user accounts or login. There is nothing to register for and nothing tied to your identity anywhere in the app.
  • No advertising. No ad network, no ad identifiers, no ad SDK.
  • No analytics or tracking SDK of any kind. No analytics library, attribution SDK, or third-party tracker is included in the app.
  • No sale or sharing of personal information. Rotunda has never sold, rented, or shared personal information with any third party, and its architecture doesn't support doing so, even if that changed: see Section 8.

3. What is stored on your device, and how to clear it

Rotunda keeps a small amount of data on your device only, using an on-device key-value store. Nothing in it is transmitted anywhere, and nothing in it is visible to Rotunda's developers: it stays under your control, on your device, for as long as you keep it there.

What's stored on-device:

  • Saved / followed bills and votes, the bills and votes you've bookmarked, so Rotunda can show your Saved list and check those items for status changes.
  • A one-time notification-permission flag, a single true/false marker recording whether Rotunda has already shown you its in-app explanation of why it wants notification permission, so you're only asked once.
  • A local cache of recently-viewed search and vote results, kept so the app can show you the same screen instantly on a re-visit and can still show your last-known results if a government source is temporarily unreachable. Cached entries expire automatically after 24 hours.

How to clear it: to remove a single saved bill or vote, open your Saved list and tap the remove control on that item; it's deleted from your device immediately. To clear everything Rotunda has stored at once, uninstall the app: all on-device storage is deleted along with it, and reinstalling starts with nothing saved.

4. Bill search: the one thing sent off your device

When you search for a bill by keyword, Rotunda sends your search text directly to GovInfo's own public search API, over HTTPS, so it can look up matching bills. That request goes straight from your device to GovInfo's servers as part of running your search: Rotunda has no server of its own for that request to pass through or be logged by. GovInfo's handling of that request is governed by GovInfo's own privacy policy, linked in Section 6 below.

Each search you run is treated as a one-off: Rotunda does not store your search history on your device beyond the short-lived result cache described in Section 3, and does not attach any identifier that would let it, or GovInfo, tie your searches together across sessions.

5. Local notifications

If you choose to enable notifications, Rotunda can notify you when a bill or vote you're following changes status. This is handled entirely on-device:

  • A background check runs periodically (roughly every 6 hours) to see whether anything you follow has changed, using the same government sources described in Section 6.
  • If something changed, Rotunda schedules a notification using your device's own local notification system.

This is a purely local, on-device schedule: it never involves a push notification service, a push token, or any server round-trip. Rotunda never sends your notification preferences, followed items, or device identifiers to anyone. You can decline the notification permission prompt, or turn notifications off at any time from your device's system Settings, and the app continues to work normally.

6. Government data sources Rotunda reads from

Every vote, bill, and document Rotunda shows you is read live, over HTTPS, directly from the U.S. government's own published sources. Rotunda does not operate a mirror, cache server, or intermediary for this data:

  • congress.gov (api.congress.gov), House recorded votes and bill metadata.
  • The U.S. Senate's own published records (senate.gov), Senate recorded votes.
  • GovInfo (api.govinfo.gov / www.govinfo.gov), the Government Publishing Office's official document repository, bill text and bill search.

These requests use a Rotunda-provisioned API key (an api.data.gov key) that identifies the app to these services for rate-limiting purposes; it does not identify you personally, and it is not tied to your device or your activity in any way Rotunda controls.

These are independent, government-operated services, and each has its own privacy practices, not Rotunda's:

  • congress.gov / Library of Congress: https://www.congress.gov/privacy-policy
  • U.S. Senate: https://www.senate.gov/general/privacy.htm
  • GovInfo / U.S. Government Publishing Office: https://www.govinfo.gov/privacy-policy

We encourage you to review those policies if you have questions about how those government services themselves handle requests.

7. Crash reporting

Rotunda includes the Sentry crash-reporting library so that crashes and errors can be diagnosed and fixed. As of this writing, crash reporting has not been configured for any release of the app, so it currently sends nothing to anyone. This is a build-time configuration state, not a promise about future versions.

If crash reporting is configured in a future version of the app, what it sends is limited to technical diagnostic information used to fix bugs: error messages, stack traces, and basic device/OS information (device model, OS version, app version). It would not include anything you type into search, the contents of your Saved list, or any other information Rotunda does not otherwise collect. This policy will be updated, and the "last updated" date above revised, if and when that changes.

8. No sale or sharing of information

Your information stays yours: Rotunda has never sold, rented, traded, or shared personal information with any third party for advertising, marketing, or any other purpose, and has no server-side infrastructure where such information could ever be collected or held for that purpose. The only outbound data flow is the search request described in Section 4, sent directly to GovInfo as part of using the search feature itself, and the government API calls described in Section 6.

9. Children's privacy

Rotunda is a general-audience civic reference app. It is not directed at children, has no account system, no age gate, and no feature that asks for or knowingly collects personal information from anyone, including children under 13. Rotunda does not have actual knowledge that any of its users are children under 13.

Under the Children's Online Privacy Protection Act (COPPA, 16 CFR Part 312), a general-audience app like Rotunda is subject to COPPA's requirements through the "actual knowledge" prong: an operator becomes obligated only once it has actual knowledge a specific user is a child under 13, not merely because the app is "likely to attract" children as a general matter (that stricter standard applies to apps directed at children, which Rotunda is not). Every user of Rotunda, of any age, uses the app the same way, without an account, a login, or any request for personal information, so this obligation is not currently triggered for any user: Rotunda collects no personal information from any user, has no mechanism to determine a user's age, and has no server-side records of any kind that could contain a child's information. If you believe a child has provided personal information through this app, contact us using the information in Section 11 and we will look into it, though as described throughout this policy, there is no mechanism by which the app itself would have collected or retained such information.

10. Your state privacy rights

A number of U.S. states (including California, under the CCPA/CPRA, and roughly 19 other states as of 2026) give residents rights such as the right to know what personal information is collected about them and the right to request deletion. Nearly all of these laws apply only to businesses that meet a revenue, user-count, or data-sale threshold. Your privacy is intact regardless of where you live: Rotunda has no revenue, sells no data, and keeps no server-side records about any user at all, so it does not meet any state's applicability threshold today.

We still want to be straightforward with you: everything Rotunda could ever "know" or "delete" on your behalf already lives on your own device (Section 3), and you can view or clear it yourself at any time, in the app or by uninstalling it. If you'd still like to reach out with a privacy question or request, see Section 11. We'll honor a reasonable request voluntarily, even though no state law currently compels us to.

11. Contact

For questions about this policy or Rotunda's privacy practices, please open an issue on the Rotunda project's GitHub Issues page (the rotunda-pages repository), which is the app's current, official support channel. This section will be revised if that channel changes, and the "last updated" date above will be updated accordingly.

12. Security

All communication between Rotunda and any external source, the government APIs in Section 6, happens exclusively over HTTPS (encrypted in transit). Data you store on your device (Section 3) is protected by your device's own operating-system-level security (e.g., device passcode/biometric lock and OS-level app sandboxing); Rotunda relies on those platform protections rather than adding its own encryption layer on top. Rotunda also has no server-side infrastructure of its own: there is no Rotunda-operated database, backend, or account system to secure or to be breached.

13. Changes to this policy

If Rotunda's data practices change, for example, if crash reporting (Section 7) is configured, or a new feature changes what is stored or where it's sent, this policy will be updated and the "last updated" date at the top will be revised to reflect that. Since Rotunda has no account system, we cannot notify you directly; we encourage you to check back periodically, and any in-app privacy policy screen will always reflect the current version of this document.
